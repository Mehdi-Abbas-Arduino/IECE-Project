Power

Power rails and current budget

USB 5 V → Pico

The Pico runs from its USB cable, which also carries the serial console. Its on-board regulator makes the 3V3 rail.

Pico W ≈ 50–100 mA with Wi-Fi off

3V3(OUT) · pin 36

  • OLED ≈ 20 mA
  • IR sensor ≈ 20 mA
  • Level shifter LV ≈ 0 mA
Total ≈ 40 mA of the 300 mA available

External 5 V

  • SG90 servo: 100–250 mA moving, ~650 mA stalled
  • HC-SR04 ≈ 15 mA
  • Level shifter HV ≈ 0 mA
Use a supply rated 1 A or more
Common ground is required

Connect the external supply's − terminal to a Pico GND pin. Without a shared ground the servo jitters and the ultrasonic readings come out as noise. Don't connect the external 5 V to VBUS while USB is plugged in.

Design notes

Why it's wired this way

Level shifter on the HC-SR04

The HC-SR04 runs at 5 V and its ECHO pin outputs 5 V. Pico GPIOs are rated for 3.3 V, so ECHO goes through the BSS138 shifter. TRIG goes through it too, so the 3.3 V trigger becomes a clean 5 V pulse.

LED resistors

I = (3.3 V − Vf) / 220 Ω. Green (Vf ≈ 2.1 V) draws about 5.5 mA and red (Vf ≈ 1.9 V) about 6.4 mA. Both are well under the Pico's 12 mA per-pin limit and bright enough indoors.

Servo signal through the shifter

GP16's 3.3 V PWM goes through channel 3 of the same level shifter, so the servo gets a full 5 V signal that matches its 5 V supply. This stops the jitter some SG90s show with a 3.3 V signal. The shifter doesn't change the timing, so main.py needs no changes. 0° (closed) is a 0.5 ms pulse (duty_u16 ≈ 1638) and 90° (open) is 1.5 ms (≈ 4915). A 470 µF capacitor across the servo's V+ and GND smooths current spikes.

IR sensor at 3.3 V

Powering the IR module from 3V3 keeps its OUT pin at Pico-safe levels. The module's on-board LED lights when it detects something. Turn its potentiometer until a car in the spot triggers it and an empty spot doesn't.

OLED on I2C0

GP4 and GP5 are the default I2C0 pins. Most 0.96″ modules sit at address 0x3C. Some are 0x3D, and test_oled.py prints the address it finds.

Sensor placement

Mount the HC-SR04 at the entrance facing the approach lane. It opens the gate when something is closer than 10 cm (GATE_TRIGGER_CM). Mount the IR sensor at the back of the spot, facing where the car stops.

Bring-up

Before you power it on

  1. With nothing powered, check that external 5 V and 3V3 are not shorted to each other or to GND.
  2. Confirm the external supply's − terminal is wired to a Pico GND pin.
  3. Check the LED polarity: long leg (anode) to the resistor, short leg to GND.
  4. Confirm ECHO reaches GP3 only through the level shifter, never directly. Check that the servo SIG wire goes to HV3, not straight to GP16.
  5. Run each test script on its own: test_leds.py, test_ir.py, test_ultrasonic.py, test_servo.py, test_oled.py.
  6. Once every part passes, run main.py.
